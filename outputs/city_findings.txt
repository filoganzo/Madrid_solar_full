  This is the same weakness the block study named, at city scale and

  obstruction is worth ~2% of yield in a city built to a cornice line.

============================================================================
5 · WHAT THIS CHANGES
============================================================================
  Section 11 of the block study extrapolates one block to Madrid and
  flags the ratio it uses as the most sensitive input in the section.
  This run replaces that assumption with a measurement over 19,648
  roofs, and the ratio moves:

    block-derived      0.97 kWp/dwelling  ->  1.11 GW
    city-measured      0.72 kWp/dwelling  ->  0.82 GW

  The honest caveat, stated as plainly as the number: this extract is
  still central Madrid, so it is a wider sample of the same kind of
  city rather than a survey of every typology in it. Vallecas and the
  post-war periphery have sloped, lower, differently-owned roofs, and
  they are still under-represented here.

  What does not move is the constraint. A larger roof per dwelling
  changes the size of the prize; it does not change who has to sign.
  The binding input is still a junta de propietarios vote, and the
  deployment rate is still set by the sales cycle rather than the sun.
